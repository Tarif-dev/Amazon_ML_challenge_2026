# Amazon ML Challenge 2026

## Business Entity Resolution — Complete Challenge Brief

---

## 1. Challenge Overview

The Amazon ML Challenge 2026 problem presented in the video is a **Business Entity Resolution** task.

The objective is to determine which business records from three independent data sources refer to the **same real-world business**.

The three sources do not share a common identifier. Therefore, the system must infer whether records represent the same entity using the information available in the provided datasets.

### Core objective

For every business entity in **Source 1**, determine:

* Whether it has no matching entity in Sources 2 or 3.
* Whether it has one matching entity.
* Whether it has multiple matching entities.

The final system should produce the complete set of matching Source 2 and Source 3 IDs for every Source 1 entity.

---

# 2. Information Available for Matching

The challenge specifically focuses on:

* **Business name**
* **Business address**

The video explicitly notes that phone numbers and email addresses may exist in real-world business data, but:

> Phone and email exist in reality. This challenge uses name and address only.

Therefore, the matching system must work with the information provided in the challenge dataset rather than relying on additional identifying information.

---

Although these records are written differently, they may represent the same real-world business.

This demonstrates the central difficulty of entity resolution:

> Different textual representations can refer to the same real-world entity.

---

# 4. Matching vs. Look-Alike Records

Not every similar-looking business record is a true match.

The challenge therefore requires the system to distinguish between:

### True matches

Records that represent the same real-world business.

For example:

`Acme Robotics Inc`

and

`Acme Robotics Incorporated`

may refer to the same entity.

Similarly:

`Acme Robotics Inc`

and

`Acme Robotics` with an address near City Hall

may represent the same business.

### Look-alike records

Records may contain similar names or addresses without representing the same business.

Therefore, similarity alone is not sufficient.

The objective is **entity-level matching**, not simply string similarity.

The two major stages are:

# 6. Blocking

## 6.1 What is Blocking?

Comparing every Source 1 record against every Source 2 and Source 3 record would be computationally expensive.

Instead, the challenge uses **blocking** to create a smaller set of potentially relevant candidate records.

The video describes blocking as grouping records using a cheap key constructed from:

> Name + Address

The purpose is to create a shortlist containing potentially matching records.

---

## 6.2 Why Blocking Matters

Blocking is extremely important because it determines the maximum possible recall of the final system.

The key principle from the video is:

> Blocking sets the recall ceiling.

If a true match is excluded during blocking, the later matching model cannot recover it.

In other words:

```text
True match
     │
     ├── excluded by blocking
     │
     ▼
Impossible to recover later
```

Therefore:

> An entity cannot be matched if it is never considered as a candidate.

This makes candidate generation one of the most important parts of the solution.

# 18. Evaluation Metric

The challenge uses:

> **Macro F0.5, precision-weighted**

The formula shown in the video is:

$$
F_{0.5}
=
\frac{1.25 \times Precision \times Recall}
{0.25 \times Precision + Recall}
$$

Equivalently:

$$
F_{\beta}
=
(1+\beta^2)
\frac{Precision \times Recall}
{\beta^2 Precision + Recall}
$$

with:

$$
\beta=0.5
$$

---

# 19. Why Precision Matters More

F0.5 places more emphasis on **precision** than recall.

This is particularly important for entity resolution because an incorrect merge can be more damaging than failing to merge two records.

The video states:

> A false merge costs about twice as much as a miss.

Therefore, the system should be conservative when the evidence for a match is weak.

The video summarizes this principle as:

> When unsure, do not merge.

This does **not** mean that recall should be ignored.

Rather, the system needs to balance:

* Finding genuine matches
* Avoiding incorrect merges

while operating under a metric that gives greater weight to precision.

# 20. Singletons

A **singleton** is a Source 1 entity that has:

> No match in Sources 2 or 3.

For these entities, the correct prediction is:

```text
empty list
```

The video states that:

> Predicting an empty list for a singleton receives 1.0.

Whereas:

> Predicting any match for a singleton scores 0.

Therefore, identifying entities that genuinely have no corresponding records is an important part of the problem.

# 21. Regional Patterns

The video explicitly highlights the importance of:

> Region-specific patterns in names and addresses.

Business names and addresses may vary depending on geographic region.

A robust system should therefore avoid assuming that every region follows exactly the same:

* Address conventions
* Abbreviations
* Naming patterns
* Formatting
* Ordering of address components

Regional variation should be considered during both:

* Blocking
* Matching

# 23. Key Challenge Principles

The complete problem can therefore be reduced to several core principles.

### Principle 1 — Resolve entities, not strings

Two records do not need to be textually identical to represent the same business.

---

### Principle 2 — Blocking comes first

The blocking stage determines which potential matches are even considered.

A missed candidate at this stage cannot be recovered later.

---

### Principle 3 — Candidate generation is not matching

A candidate pair is only a possibility.

The matching stage must determine whether the candidate is actually the same real-world entity.

---

### Principle 4 — Precision matters heavily

The evaluation metric is F0.5, which gives greater weight to precision.

False merges are particularly costly.

---

### Principle 5 — No-match predictions matter

Some Source 1 entities genuinely have no corresponding entity in Sources 2 or 3.

These must be represented with an empty list.

---

### Principle 6 — Use only challenge data

External databases, APIs, and geocoding services cannot be used.

---

### Principle 7 — Regional variation matters

Name and address normalization/matching should account for regional patterns.
